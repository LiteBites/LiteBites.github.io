## Free is the headline; eligibility is the first filter

Kiro’s student offer is straightforward on the surface: eligible university students receive 1,000 credits every month for one year at no cost. There is no credit card requirement, and Kiro says the tier is intended for class projects, research, hackathons, and personal ventures.

The first catch is that “student” does not mean universally eligible. Kiro publishes a list of participating universities, and the offer currently depends on attending one of them. Students whose school is missing can submit a university-affiliated email to request consideration, but that is a request for expansion, not immediate access.

The signup flow also has a specific trust boundary. Create or connect a Kiro account with a university email, then verify student status through SheerID from the account usage page. Google, GitHub, or AWS Builder ID can be used for sign-in, but the account still needs the university-affiliated address. Kiro also says applicants must be at least 18.

## What 1,000 credits actually means

The offer is not an unlimited free version of Kiro Pro. Credits do not roll over, add-on credits are unavailable on the student tier, and reaching the monthly limit means waiting for the next cycle. That makes the offer predictable, but not infinitely expandable: a heavy agent workflow can consume the month’s allowance before a large project is finished.

Kiro presents the student experience around spec-driven development, AI agents, plain-English building, and terminal workflows. Those features may make the student tier useful for learning an AI-assisted engineering workflow rather than merely generating snippets. But the official page’s student testimonials are marketing evidence, not independent measurements of productivity or code quality. Students should treat claims such as building a RAG chatbot in a day as personal anecdotes, not expected results.

The page also includes a data-control detail worth reading before signing up. Kiro says users can opt out of having their content used for model training through the mechanism described in its privacy documentation. That is a setting to inspect, not a reason to assume that every account has identical data handling by default.

## The decision after the free year

When the one-year student period ends, Kiro says the account automatically moves to its free tier and the user does not lose work. Code, specs, and projects remain on the user’s machine and in repositories. The offer therefore has a relatively clear exit path: students can continue at the lower tier or choose a paid plan if 1,000 monthly credits was not enough.

Before enrolling, check five things: whether your university is listed, whether you can use its affiliated email, whether you are 18 or older, how your normal tasks fit inside 1,000 non-rolling credits, and which data-sharing settings you want enabled. The price is attractive, but the offer is best understood as a one-year learning budget with a monthly ceiling—not a promise of unlimited AI coding.
