# Adapting the Paper Bite for Position Papers

Use this reference when a paper argues a thesis but introduces no new controlled benchmark, trained system, or experimental table.

## Evidence classification

Classify each public claim before drafting:

| Evidence type | Safe wording |
|---|---|
| Position/value statement | “The authors argue…” or “The paper’s position is…” |
| Result cited from prior work | “The paper cites prior work reporting…” |
| Qualitative case analysis | “The authors assess…” |
| Numerical estimate | “The paper estimates…”; state that it is not measured |
| Proposed workflow/algorithm | “The paper outlines…”; do not imply validation |
| Acknowledged counterview | “The authors acknowledge…” |
| LiteBites interpretation | “A useful way to read this is…” |

Do not turn literature synthesis into a result produced by the current paper. When several cited studies use different models, tasks, hardware, or metrics, do not merge their numbers into one standardized benchmark claim.

## Six-section adaptation

- **Why this paper matters:** explain the operational decision or debate.
- **The bite:** state the thesis and its scope qualifier, such as “many calls, not all calls.”
- **How it works:** explain the proposed architecture, taxonomy, or migration process.
- **What to look at in the results:** open by stating that the paper is non-experimental. Describe the evidence mix, the strongest counterargument, and what remains unvalidated.
- **Practical takeaways:** turn the thesis into hypotheses, measurements, or decision gates a practitioner can test.
- **Links:** use a versioned paper URL, official project/discussion page, and omit code when none exists.

## Figure rule

Use one explanatory source figure if it clarifies the thesis or workflow. Omit a second figure when no experimental figure exists. Never manufacture a result chart, screenshot body text, or use a decorative project image to satisfy a template.

## Quality gate

Before finalizing, verify that the post answers all four:

1. What exactly is the paper claiming?
2. What evidence is original to this paper versus cited from elsewhere?
3. Which numbers are estimates rather than measurements?
4. What observation or experiment would falsify or strengthen the position?
